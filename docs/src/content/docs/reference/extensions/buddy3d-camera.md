Buddy3D camera is the server a Buddy3D camera talks to in place of Prusa's cloud. The camera sends its pictures to this server, and they show on the camera's printer. The live view is a WebRTC connection from the camera straight to the browser, so the video never passes through the server. Nothing leaves your network.

To use it:

1. Install it and approve the port it listens on: TCP `9443`, where cameras send their pictures. In a container, publish it, for example `9443:9443`.
2. On a printer's Cameras tab, add a camera of the type Buddy3D camera, and pair it in the section under the camera.
3. Type the Wi-Fi the camera joins, unless it keeps the one it has. Download `local_cfg.tar`, copy it to the root of the camera's SD card and start the camera. The camera needs firmware 3.1.7 or later.

Once the camera connects, the section under it has three tabs. Control turns the camera and sets its infrared and video quality. Settings sets how often it sends a picture, the stream it runs, its timelapse and its speaker volume. Information shows its firmware, network and SD card. Changing anything takes a person who may operate the camera.

A camera with the wrong time does not work, and says nothing about it. The extension is the cameras' time server by default, and its settings page says when the time server does not answer.

The README in `extensions/buddy3d-camera` describes the camera's protocol, the settings and the time server.

## What it adds {#what-it-adds}

- **Buddy3D cameras**: a tab on the module's page under Settings, Extensions
- **Buddy3D camera**: a section under each camera of a printer
- **Buddy3D camera**: the live view of a camera of a type it offers

## What it needs {#what-it-needs}

- the server methods `access.check`, `camera.accept`
- the permissions `observe`, `operate`, approved at install
- the TCP port 9443, `camera`, approved at install, for Buddy3D cameras send their pictures and open their sessions here
- a data directory of its own, which an update and a removal keep

## Settings {#settings}

An operator writes them under `config:` in the extension's entry of the configuration.

| key | type | default | what it sets |
|---|---|---|---|
| `address` | string | none | The address a camera dials and the certificate names. Empty is this machine's first IPv4 address. |
| `snapshot_interval` | int | `30` | How often a camera sends a picture, in seconds, from 10 to 254. |
